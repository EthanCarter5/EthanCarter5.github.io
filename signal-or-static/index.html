Is your drill repeatable?

This checks one thing: does the same player get the same number twice?

If yes, the drill gives a real number and you can compare players with it. If no, you're ranking players on luck. It does not tell you who is good or whether the drill predicts the NFL.

Example: 20 players run a shuttle twice. For each player you enter his time from run 1 and from run 2. That's all.

Verdict

What makes a drill trustworthy
  • Stops last half a second or more. The trackers record 10 times a second, so shorter stops are only 2 or 3 frames and read too low.
  • Start on a cue, like the snap, not when the player decides to go. Snap starts and self-starts give different get-off times.
  • Run each player at least twice, same day, same setup. One run can't be checked.
  • Test with 20 or more players before you trust the verdict.
  • Measure top speed where the player runs straight and free. The 40's top speed repeats at 88% to 94% at every position. Stops and turns inside short, curved or contact drills repeat much less.
Data: NFL Big Data Bowl 2027 Combine tracking. Each verdict comes from Signal Share, the share of the differences between players that is real (a one-way intraclass correlation across players with 2+ reps; 95% ranges from 1,000 bootstrap resamples). Trust it at 80% or more, mostly trust at 60%, use with caution at 40%, don't use below that. A real number can still fail to predict the NFL, but a noisy one can't.
